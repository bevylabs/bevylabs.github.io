How to count PL more accurately

Counting by weight

  1. Scoop a small sample and blot it on a wet cloth for the same few seconds every time.
  2. Weigh it on a jewellery scale (0.01 g). About 1 g is enough.
  3. Count every PL in the sample. Count each one; don't estimate.
  4. Do two or three samples and use the average PL per gram.
  5. Weigh the batch the same way and multiply.

The biggest error is water. A few drops left on a 1 g sample can change PL per gram a lot, and that error is multiplied across the whole batch.

Counting by eye

Spread a few hundred PL in a shallow white bowl or tray and count with a hand tally counter. It is accurate when the counter is fresh, but packing often happens in the early morning hours, and tired eyes miss PL.

For farmers receiving seed

ICAR-CIBA advises counting a bag or two of the seed you receive instead of trusting only the invoice. Then you know the real number you are stocking, bonus included.

Questions

How do you count shrimp seed (PL) by weight?

Blot and weigh a small sample, count every PL in it, and divide the count by the weight to get PL per gram. Multiply by the weight of the whole batch. Use the average of two or three samples.

Why is a weighed PL count often wrong?

Water left on the sample adds weight without adding PL, which lowers PL per gram, and the error grows across the whole batch. Blot the same way every time and count more than one sample.

What is the bonus in shrimp seed sales?

Extra seed given free on top of the invoiced number to cover transport and acclimation losses. In India it is commonly 30 to 50 percent. An inaccurate count means the hatchery gives away more than it meant to, or the farmer stocks a different number than planned.

Should farmers count the seed they receive?

Yes. ICAR-CIBA advises counting a bag or two of the supplied seed so the stocking density is what you planned.
