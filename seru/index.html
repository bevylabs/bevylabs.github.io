SERU questions people ask

What is the SERU assessment?

SERU stands for Safety, Equality and Regulatory Understanding. Transport for London (TfL) requires London private hire (PHV, often called PCO) drivers to pass it. It tests what is in TfL's PHV Driver's Handbook: licensing, private hire journeys, staying safe, driver behaviour, driving and parking in London, safer driving, equality and disability, safeguarding and ridesharing.

What is the SERU test format?

It is a computer-based test taken at TfL, with multiple choice questions and fill-in-the-gap sentences. It is widely reported as 37 questions in 45 minutes, with 60% needed to pass. Check TfL's SERU page for the latest details before your test.

How many people pass SERU?

Fewer than you might think. Freedom of information figures reported by Taxi-Point showed that only 54% of private hire applicants passed between October 2025 and April 2026. Practising with timed mock tests and reviewing your wrong answers makes a real difference.

How should I prepare for SERU?

Read the PHV Driver's Handbook one section at a time, then practise questions on that section straight away. Once you are scoring well, take full timed mock tests (37 questions, 45 minutes) and go back over every question you got wrong until you understand why the right answer is right.

Can I get SERU explanations in Urdu, Bengali, Arabic or Romanian?
